# Source analysis protocol

Complete this protocol for all five uploaded documents before reaching alignment or recommendation conclusions. Use S1–S5 as defined in SKILL.md. Read every relevant page, table, diagram, and appendix; inspect page images when extraction obscures notation. Record available/readable status and reproducible locators. If any required source is missing or unreadable, identify it clearly, request the specific missing material, and STOP. Do not perform a partial review. Complete intake verification before analysis; if necessary information is missing later, stop dependent analysis until resolved. Do not fill gaps using memory, assumptions, or external documents.

## Source evidence inventory

Record title, date/version when stated, grade level, unit when applicable, and compatibility across sources. Distinguish a field not stated from one not applicable.

- **S1 GaDOE Unit:** Enumerate every standard and substandard exactly as provided, with code, description, locator, content/skill, and mastery expectations. Include explicit practices or cross-cutting expectations and distinguish instructional guidance from assessment requirements. Do not omit standards because the current CUA does not assess them.
- **S2 GMAS Blueprint:** Extract relevant expectations, domains, item types, weighting, and DOK information when available. Record denominators and scope for weights. Explicitly mark information absent from the upload. Grade-wide weights are context, not automatic unit-CUA quotas.
- **S3 ALDs:** Capture relevant Beginning, Developing, Proficient, and Distinguished expectations when available, including their source wording or faithful summaries and locators. Mark missing levels or content explicitly. Do not assign achievement levels to individual students or equate an ALD level with DOK.
- **S4 DRC BEACON:** Extract documented, relevant content, mastery, cognitive-demand, format, or reporting expectations and their applicability to this unit. Separate source statements from reviewer interpretations. Do not assume BEACON rules govern a CUA.
- **S5 Current CUA:** Inventory every original question and subpart, directions, visuals, stated points, and available keys/rubrics. Preserve numbering; assign stable review IDs only when needed. Map each question to the S1 standard/substandard and content it actually assesses, identify item type, and estimate DOK with rationale and uncertainty. Record unmatched or uncertain mappings explicitly.

## DOK estimation

Use Webb's Depth of Knowledge as an explicitly identified reviewer framework: DOK 1 recall/routine execution, DOK 2 skills and concepts involving decisions, DOK 3 strategic reasoning and justification, DOK 4 extended synthesis/investigation. Estimate the thinking required by the actual task, not its length, numerical complexity, verbs, format, or apparent difficulty. Routine multistep work is not automatically DOK 3; a short question is not automatically DOK 1. Note alternative demands if scaffolding or unclear wording changes the classification. Do not claim the estimate is an official source classification. Compare against source-stated DOK only when provided and applicable; never invent a required distribution.

## Two scales of evaluation

Maintain a standards/substandards ledger covering every S1 entry, linked to original question IDs or marked unassessed. Evaluate each question using question-review-protocol.md. Also evaluate the complete CUA for standards coverage, depth of mastery evidence, reasoning/DOK and item-type balance, applicable BEACON contributions, ALD opportunities, gaps, and unnecessary redundancy. Distinguish useful repetition across representations or reasoning demands from duplication that adds little evidence. Use explicit item and point denominators, preserve unknown points, and disclose overlapping mappings rather than double-counting silently.

Reach conclusions only after reconciling the five-source inventory, question matrix, and whole-assessment ledger. Cite uploaded evidence for substantive conclusions; distinguish documented expectations, estimates, and professional judgments.
